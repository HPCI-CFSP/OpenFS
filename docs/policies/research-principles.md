# Research Principles

1. Prefer primary and official sources for factual claims.
2. Preserve original wording, date, scope, units, and measurement conditions in the evidence record.
3. Separate observation, vendor claim, third-party analysis, forecast, interpretation, and recommendation.
4. Record contrary evidence and unresolved uncertainty.
5. Treat missing evidence as unknown, not false.
6. Measure coverage against a declared monitor scope; never claim complete coverage of the Web.
7. Do not use the number of derivative articles as independent corroboration.
8. Prefer reproducible, structured transformations for deduplication, validation, scoring, and promotion.
9. Make every material report statement traceable to accepted claims and decisions.
10. Revisit dependent knowledge when a source is updated, corrected, retracted, or becomes stale.
11. Search for and reflect material public developments as soon as operational
    capacity and the verified security profile allow. A fixed calendar is a
    minimum backstop, not a reason to postpone a high-impact official release.
12. Use two publication lanes: publish a clearly labelled provisional update
    after source, boundary, and schema checks; promote it only after independent
    validation and the applicable Consensus and human-decision gates.
13. Publicly report what was last checked, what changed, verification state, and
    Coverage Gaps. Keep internal Monitor cadence dynamic and do not imply that a
    search found all Web information.
